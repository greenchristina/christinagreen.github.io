Calculating Developed vs. Undeveloped Land

JCPRD master plan project · ArcGIS Pro

  • Land Classification
  • Spatial Data Analysis

For this master plan project with JCPRD, I tackled a unique spatial analysis challenge: creating a metric to accurately classify developed versus undeveloped land across our park system. In standard municipal planning, “developed” usually just means impervious surfaces like pavement, while undeveloped land is left natural. But for parks, that definition falls short.

Take prairie restorations, for example. Prairies used to cover Kansas, but because of changing climate conditions, JCPRD’s restored prairies can’t survive independently—they require active, ongoing human management to stay healthy. Because of that upkeep, the district technically classifies them as developed land.

Using ArcGIS Pro, I researched and built out a custom classification metric that factored in these operational realities. It gave the district a much more accurate way to measure their land-use ratios and see how we stacked up against other parks and recreation departments.
