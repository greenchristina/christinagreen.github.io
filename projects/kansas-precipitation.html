Kansas Precipitation Variability Map

Cartography class project · ArcGIS Pro · Excel · Arcade

  • Spatial Data Analysis

For this cartography class project, I wanted to experiment with some advanced design techniques to map out precipitation patterns across Kansas. I pulled annual county-level precipitation data from 2014 to 2024 provided by the Kansas Office of the State Climatologist. After wrangling all the data into Excel, I calculated the mean and standard deviation of rainfall for every county and derived the coefficient of variation (CV) to show how much precipitation fluctuates over time.

To get the labeling right in ArcGIS Pro, I wrote an Arcade expression to automatically filter and display only cities with a population over 126,000. That created a few design roadblocks—for instance, I had to manually remove “Overland Park” because its label was getting squished between Kansas City and Olathe. On the flip side, filtering by population left western Kansas looking totally empty, so I manually added “Hays” and “Dodge City” to give the map better geographic context. Later on, when I created a special callout for Seward County (which had the highest rainfall variation), I dropped “Liberal” into the callout box so viewers would have a familiar city anchor right there on the map.
